\subsection{Natural-image data and covariance}
\label{sec:app-methods-images}
% Used by: Fig.~\ref{fig:phenomena} (bottom), Fig.~\ref{fig:pixel},
% Fig.~\ref{fig:feature}, App.~\ref{sec:app-ext-pixel}, \ref{sec:app-ext-gauge}.
% Source: CR/scripts/validate_vanhateren_disk_teacher.py (load_vanhateren_image,
% stage_vanhateren_uint8, compute_population_spectrum; staged 2026-08-18, log
% CR/logs/vanhateren_*); spectrum cache CR/tables/vanhateren_disk_teacher_spectrum.npz;
% disk teacher CR/scripts/validate_ffhq_disk_teacher.py:create_disk_teacher.
% Numbers below recomputed from the spectrum cache.
All pixel-space and linear-feature experiments on natural images (Figs.~\ref{fig:pixel}
and~\ref{fig:feature}; Apps.~\ref{sec:app-ext-pixel}, \ref{sec:app-ext-gauge})
share one image set, covariance and teacher.  The toy example of
Fig.~\ref{fig:phenomena} uses a separate, simpler pipeline
(App.~\ref{sec:app-methods-toy}).

\paragraph{Images and patches.}
We used the uncalibrated van Hateren natural-image database (\texttt{.iml}
files, $1024\times1536$ pixels, 16-bit big-endian luminance $L$).  Each image
was mapped to $\operatorname{round}\!\big(255\log(1+L)/\log 65536\big)/255\in[0,1]$,
i.e.\ log luminance quantized to eight bits.  From 4,000 randomly chosen
source images we drew $100\times100$ patches at uniformly random positions: a
\emph{training pool} of 2,000 patches (one from each of 2,000 images) and a
\emph{population pool} of 20,000 patches (ten from each of the other 2,000
images).  The two pools share no source image, so fitted models are always
evaluated on images from different photographs.  One crop per training image
keeps training samples independent: with 25 crops per image, correlated
sibling crops leaked through leave-one-out cross-validation, which then chose
the interpolating boundary of the penalty grid in 26 of 100 trials already
at $\sigma^2/S=0.03$.  Patches are vectorized in $\R^{d}$, $d=10^4$.

\paragraph{Population covariance.}
The population pool was centered once, and its empirical covariance
$\Sigma=\frac1{20{,}000}P^\top P$ ($P$: centered patch matrix) was
diagonalized on a GPU in single precision, $\Sigma=\sum_ks_k\uvec_k\uvec_k^\top$.
% NB (issue #26): covariance and eigh are computed in float32 in
% compute_population_spectrum; tail eigenvalues (~2e-6) are near float32
% resolution (eps*s_1 ~ 3.7e-6).  Recompute in float64 before final.
With 20,000 samples in $d=10^4$ dimensions all eigenvalues are positive; they span
7.2 decades ($s_1=30.7$, $s_d=2.0\times10^{-6}$, $\operatorname{Tr}\Sigma=46.9$).
The leading PC, essentially mean luminance, carries 65\% of the total
variance, the top 100 PCs 89\%.  This $\Sigma$ serves both as the covariance
entering every deterministic equivalent and as the evaluation distribution for
$E_{gen}$.

\paragraph{Disk teacher.}
The teacher $\bstar$ is the indicator of a centered disk of radius $0.3$ on
the $[-1,1]^2$ patch grid (radius $\approx15$ pixels; 692 pixels, so
$\|\bstar\|^2=692$), and $b_k=\uvec_k^\top\bstar$.  Its natural response
variance is $S=\sum_ks_kb_k^2=1736.34$.  The teacher is strongly structured
relative to the spectrum: the first PC carries 90.7\% of $S$ but only 7.4\% of
$\|\bstar\|^2$, and PCs beyond the 100th carry 13.8\% of $\|\bstar\|^2$ while
contributing $0.04\%$ of $S$.  It is this Euclidean teacher energy in
low-variance directions that prediction barely weights and accentuation
exposes.

The linear example is a construction rather than a fit: it isolates the
geometry of Sec.~\ref{sec:setup} with two students that differ from the
teacher only along low-variance directions.

\paragraph{Images and covariance.}
We drew 12,000 random $100\times100$ patches from 500 van Hateren images
(image and patch positions fixed by seed 42).  Each source image was min--max
normalized to $[0,1]$ and a constant $0.15$ was subtracted; unlike
App.~\ref{sec:app-methods-images}, no log transform was applied.  The
eigenvectors $\uvec_k$ (ordered by decreasing variance) were obtained from
the SVD of the centered patch matrix.

\paragraph{Teacher and students.}
The teacher $\bstar$ is the same radius-$0.3$ disk as in
App.~\ref{sec:app-methods-images}.  For an index slice $K$ of eigenvectors let
$u_K\propto\sum_{k\in K}(\uvec_k^\top\bstar)\uvec_k$ be the unit-norm projection
of the teacher onto their span.  The two students are
\[
w_1=\bstar+\alpha_1u_{K_1},\qquad w_2=\bstar+\alpha_2u_{K_2},
\]
with $K_1$ the 400 eigenvectors ranked $d-499$ to $d-100$ (moderately low
variance; $d=10^4$), $\alpha_1=1$, and $K_2$ the 100 lowest-variance eigenvectors,
$\alpha_2=100$.  Since $\|\bstar\|\approx26$, $w_1$ is visually
indistinguishable from the teacher while $w_2$ carries a large
high-spatial-frequency component (Fig.~\ref{fig:phenomena}C.II).  Both
perturbations are nearly invisible to prediction: on the natural patches
$1-\Var(f_i-f^\star)/\Var(f^\star)$ equals $1.00000$ to five decimals for both
students (Fig.~\ref{fig:phenomena}C.I), although $\|w_2\|\approx104$ is four
times $\|\bstar\|$.

\paragraph{Accentuation.}
For a linear model $f(\x)=w^\top\x$, gradient ascent to a target $t$ from a
seed $\x_0$ has the closed-form endpoint $\x=\x_0+\lambda w$ with
$\lambda=(t-w^\top\x_0)/\|w\|^2$, so each student reaches every target
exactly on its own path.  Targets were 13 evenly spaced levels between
$\mu\pm3\sigma$ of the teacher response on natural patches
($-309.6$ to $289.8$), and seeds were 20 natural patches (seed 7); unlike
the analysis of Sec.~\ref{sec:setup}, seeds are natural images rather than
the null seed.  Figure~\ref{fig:phenomena}C.II shows every third target for
one seed ($f^\star(\x_0)=-31.4$).

\paragraph{Peer review.}
Each student's accentuated images were evaluated by the teacher and by both
students, giving the $2\times2$ generator--evaluator panels of
Fig.~\ref{fig:phenomena}C.III (all 20 seeds $\times$ 13 targets; colors mark
seeds).  Throughout Fig.~\ref{fig:phenomena}C we report
$R^2=1-\Var(f^\star-f_e)/\Var(f^\star)$, where $f^\star$ is the teacher and $f_e$
the evaluating model on the same images, i.e.\ the paper's convention of
normalizing by the variance of the true response
(Table~\ref{tab:evaluation-metrics}), together with the squared Pearson
correlation $r^2$.  Each student predicts the teacher's response to the other's accentuated
images almost perfectly ($R^2=1.0000$ and $0.9966$), and $w_1$ also controls
through its own images ($R^2=1.0000$), whereas $w_2$'s own images barely move the teacher
($R^2=-9.94$, $r^2=0.05$).  These values come from a deterministic
reproduction of the original run.

\subsection{Two-dimensional geometry illustration}
\label{sec:app-methods-geometry}
% Used by: Fig.~\ref{fig:geometry}, Fig.~\ref{fig:supp-ridge-path-geometry}.
% Source: CR/scripts/plot_ridge_paths_in_geometry.py (defaults; commits
% 0fe8c5b, 44dd89f), iso-set helpers CR/scripts/plot_iso_error_geometry.py;
% caches CR/tables/ridge_paths_iso_error_geometry.csv, ridge_gaussian_ellipses.csv.
% Main Fig. 2 = panel (b) of the script figure, restyled.
\paragraph{Setting.}
Figures~\ref{fig:geometry} and~\ref{fig:supp-ridge-path-geometry} use $d=2$
with $\Sigma=\operatorname{diag}(1,\varepsilon)$, $\varepsilon=0.04$, so the
second coordinate is a low-variance direction, and teacher
$\bstar=(1.1,0.65)^\top$, giving $S={\bstar}^\top\Sigma\bstar=1.2269$.  One
Gaussian design $X\in\R^{40\times2}$ with rows $\mathcal N(0,\Sigma)$ is drawn
once (seed 20260918) and held fixed.  With $n=40\gg d$ the effective penalty
is close to the nominal one ($\kappa=0.0518$ at $\lambda=0.05$); the figures
therefore label paths by $\lambda$ in the convention of Eq.~\ref{eq:ridge}.

\paragraph{Exact conditional law.}
For fixed $X$ and $\mathbf y=X\bstar+\sigma\eta$, the ridge estimator is exactly
Gaussian,
\[
\bhat\mid X\sim\mathcal N\big(A_\lambda X\bstar,\ \sigma^2A_\lambda A_\lambda^\top\big),
\qquad A_\lambda=(X^\top X+n\lambda I)^{-1}X^\top .
\]
Ellipses are its 68\% probability regions (Mahalanobis radius
$\sqrt{-2\log0.32}$); the dashed path is the conditional mean $A_\lambda X\bstar$
over $\lambda$, i.e.\ the noise average for this design, not the
deterministic equivalent $\bar\beta_\kappa$ of Eq.~\ref{eq:pixel-mean-weight}.
The purple path is one noise realization $\eta$, kept fixed along both sweeps
so that the path is continuous; it was chosen among four draws for visible
motion along the low-variance axis.  250 further draws verify the ellipses.
Panel (a) fixes $\lambda=0.05$ and varies $\sigma^2$ on 81 log-spaced values in
$[10^{-4},4]$ (clouds at $\sigma^2=0.01,0.1,1,4$); panel (b) fixes
$\sigma^2=0.5$ and varies $\lambda$ on 101 log-spaced values in
$[10^{-4},10]$ (clouds at $\lambda=10^{-4},10^{-2},1,10$ and at the value
$\lambda_\times=\sqrt{\det X^\top X}/n$ where the covariance ellipse swaps its
major axis).

\paragraph{Equal-error sets and marked penalties.}
Prediction contours are the ellipses $\Delta^\top\Sigma\Delta=E$ for
$E=0.01,0.04,0.09$ ($R^2_{gen}=0.99,0.97,0.93$); control contours are the circles
${\bhat}^\top\bhat-(1+z){\bhat}^\top\bstar=0$ through the origin and
$(1+z)\bstar$ for $z=-1/3,0,1$ ($R^2_{acc}=1-z^2=0.89,1,0$)
(Prop.~\ref{prop:mr-iso-geometry}).  For the plotted realization, the
prediction-stationary penalty $\lambda_{\rm pred}=0.0177$ minimizes
$E_{gen}(\bhat(\lambda))$ (golden-section search in $\log\lambda$), and the
control-calibrated penalty $\lambda_{\rm ctrl}=0.0258$ is the root of
${\bhat}^\top(\bhat-\bstar)=0$ ($z=0$) closest to $\lambda_{\rm pred}$
(bisection in $\log\lambda$).  Both are oracle quantities for this draw,
illustrating what cross-validation targets on average rather than a
cross-validated selection.

\subsection{Linear feature maps and prediction-null rotations}
\label{sec:app-methods-feature}
% Used by: Fig.~\ref{fig:feature}B--D, App.~\ref{sec:app-ext-gauge}.
% Theory of the construction: App.~\ref{sec:app-gauge-fragility}
% (whitened coordinates, right rotations with a fixed point,
% Cor.~\ref{cor:mr-rotation}).
% Content: base map = top-p population-PC projector (p = 500 main, 100 alt.);
% n = 1000; rowwise Givens rotations from high-variance to tail PCs, tail
% loading t = sin^2 theta; random family: 300 randomized rowwise rotations and
% pairings; noise sigma^2/S in {0.01, 0.1, 1, 10}; DE-selected alpha vs.\
% empirical CV; MC trials; computation of T_F.
% Sources: notebooks/README_top_pc_rotation.md, top_pc_rotation_experiment.ipynb,
% tables/vanhateren_top500_*, tables/vanhateren_null_*.
All feature-map experiments used the population covariance and disk-teacher
alignment estimated in Section~\ref{sec:app-methods-images}.  The finite-sample
designs were Gaussian with these measured second moments: if
$\Sigma=U\operatorname{diag}(s_1,\ldots,s_d)U^\top$, with eigenvalues in
decreasing order, we sampled independent population-PC scores and generated
$y={\bstar}^\top x+\sigma\epsilon$.  Thus natural images determined the
spectrum and teacher alignment, whereas the Monte Carlo experiments tested
the Gaussian theory under controlled finite-sample sampling.  Unless stated
otherwise, $d=10{,}000$, $n=1000$, and
$\sigma^2/S\in\{0.01,0.1,1,10\}$, where
$S={\bstar}^\top\Sigma\bstar$.

\paragraph{Top-PC feature map and continuous null rotation.}
The reference map $F(0)$ returned the first $p$ population-PC coordinates,
with $p=500$ in the main experiment and $p=100$ in the companion experiment.
Its feature covariance and feature--teacher covariance were
\begin{align*}
 \Sigma_F&=F(0)\Sigma F(0)^\top
 =\operatorname{diag}(s_1,\ldots,s_p),\\
 F(0)\Sigma\bstar
 &=(s_1\beta_1^\star,\ldots,s_p\beta_p^\star)^\top .
\end{align*}
The unrepresented teacher power was treated as the independent residual
variance $S_\perp$.  We constructed prediction-equivalent maps in whitened
coordinates.
Writing $A=F\Sigma^{1/2}$,
$b=\Sigma^{1/2}\bstar$, and $c=\Sigma^{-1/2}\bstar$, we decomposed the
top-PC map as $A(0)=P+R$, where $P$ is its projection onto
$\operatorname{span}(b,c)$ and $R$ is orthogonal to both constraints.  We then
formed a row frame $V$ supported on the bottom $40\%$ of the image spectrum,
also orthogonal to $b$ and $c$, and normalized it so that
$VV^\top=RR^\top$ and all cross-Gram terms vanish.  The continuous path was
\begin{equation}
 A(t)=P+\sqrt{1-t}\,R+\sqrt{t}\,V,
 \qquad F(t)=A(t)\Sigma^{-1/2},
 \qquad t=\sin^2\theta\in[0,1].
 \label{eq:app-numerical-null-rotation}
\end{equation}
It preserves, to numerical precision,
\begin{equation}
 F(t)\Sigma F(t)^\top=\operatorname{diag}(s_1,\ldots,s_p),\quad
 F(t)\Sigma\bstar=F(0)\Sigma\bstar,\quad
 F(t)\bstar=F(0)\bstar,
 \label{eq:app-numerical-null-invariants}
\end{equation}
while changing $G(t)=F(t)F(t)^\top$.  The first two equalities fix the entire
Gaussian prediction problem; the third additionally fixes the control
numerator, isolating the effect of gradient geometry in the control
denominator.  We evaluated $t=0$ and 61 logarithmically spaced values from
$10^{-6}$ to $1$.  The tail frames and all Monte Carlo draws were generated
from fixed seeds.

\paragraph{Prediction-selected penalty and Monte Carlo.}
For each $(p,\sigma^2/S)$, we evaluated 281 unnormalized ridge penalties
$\alpha\in[10^{-4},10^5]$ on a logarithmic grid and selected the minimizer of
the deterministic-equivalent leave-one-out prediction risk.  The normalized
penalty is $\lambda=\alpha/n$, and $\kappa$ was obtained from the fixed-point
equation on the invariant feature spectrum.  This selection uses the
population spectrum and teacher alignment and is therefore a theoretical
prediction-optimal benchmark, not empirical cross-validation.  The selected
penalty was fixed across rotations and Monte Carlo trials.

Finite-$n$ ridge fits were sampled exactly from the Gaussian feature design.
Because Eq.~\ref{eq:app-numerical-null-invariants} makes the joint law of
$(F(t)x,y)$ identical for every $t$, a trial used one ridge estimate for all
rotations and evaluated it under each $G(t)$.  This pairing removes irrelevant
Monte Carlo variation when comparing control geometries.  We used 300 trials
for $p=500$ and 1000 trials for $p=100$.  Figure markers report trial means
except for $R^2_{\rm acc}$, whose heavy lower tail motivated the trial median;
curves are leading deterministic equivalents.  Population metrics were
computed from the known Gaussian moments using the definitions in
Section~\ref{sec:setup}.

For every rotation and selected $\kappa$, we evaluated the control-variance
geometry directly as
\begin{equation}
 T_{F(t)}(\kappa)=\operatorname{Tr}\!\left[
 G(t)(\Sigma_F+\kappa I)^{-2}\Sigma_F\right].
 \label{eq:app-numerical-tf}
\end{equation}
Because $\Sigma_F$ is fixed along the path, changes in this trace arise only
from the pixel-gradient Gram $G(t)$.

\paragraph{Held-out empirical-CV robustness experiment.}
To verify that the result was not an artifact of DE-based penalty selection,
we repeated the $p=500$ experiment with an explicit validation set.  Each of
30 paired trials contained 1000 training and 500 validation examples.  The
underlying Gaussian inputs, teacher responses, response noise, and split were
shared across rotations, but applying $F(t)$ produced a different realized
feature matrix at every rotation.  We therefore selected $\alpha$ separately
for every trial, rotation, and noise level by minimizing held-out prediction
MSE over 71 logarithmically spaced values from $10^{-2}$ to $10^5$.  No
teacher coefficient, population risk, or control outcome entered this
selection.  Only afterward did we use the known population moments to evaluate
$E_{\rm gen}$, $E_{\rm acc}$, $R^2_{\rm gen}$, and $R^2_{\rm acc}$.  We report
trial medians and 10--90\% intervals.  The selected $\alpha$ values were also
mapped through the fixed-point equation to trial-specific $\kappa$ values for
Eq.~\ref{eq:app-numerical-tf}.  The paired design isolates the rotation effect,
while separate validation selection allows finite-sample Gram matrices and CV
choices to differ across otherwise prediction-equivalent maps.

\paragraph{Random maps.}
The random-family experiment used a canonicalized $p=500$ problem with
$\Sigma_F=I_p$ and the realizable teacher aligned to the first feature axis.
Of the remaining 499 joint-null rows, 300 were rotated and 199 were fixed.
Each rotatable high-variance row was a unit direction supported on three PCs
drawn from ranks $0.002d$--$0.15d$ and orthogonal to both $b$ and $c$; its
tail counterpart was constructed analogously from ranks $0.60d$--$d$.  Fixed
rows drew one coordinate from each intermediate band $0.15d$--$0.30d$,
$0.30d$--$0.45d$, and $0.45d$--$0.60d$.  For each of 300 random maps we
sampled a global tail propensity $u\sim\operatorname{Unif}(0,1)$, independent
row loadings $t_j\sim\operatorname{Beta}(1+12u,1+12(1-u))$, and a random
high--tail pairing.  Every draw retained the same
$(\Sigma_F,F\Sigma\bstar,F\bstar)$ but produced a different diagonal spectrum
of $G$.  We evaluated the maps using 2000 shared exact Gaussian ridge trials
at each of the four noise levels.

\paragraph{Single-row rotation.}
Fig.~\ref{fig:supp-null-givens} uses the smallest version of the
construction, $p=16$ with $\Sigma_F=I_p$.  The first row is the teacher axis
$b/\sqrt S$ in whitened coordinates.  The second row rotates in one plane,
$\cos\theta\,u_{\rm high}+\sin\theta\,u_{\rm low}$, where $u_{\rm high}$ and
$u_{\rm low}$ are unit directions orthogonal to $b$ and $c$, supported on the
PC-rank bands $0.2$--$3\%$ and $60$--$95\%$ of the spectrum, and chosen to
minimize and maximize the inverse-variance Rayleigh quotient within their
bands (dominant PC ranks 21 and 9500).  The remaining 14 rows are random
orthonormal directions in the band $3$--$15\%$, also orthogonal to $b$ and
$c$, and fixed along the path.  Every angle therefore satisfies
Eq.~\ref{eq:app-numerical-null-invariants}, and only the second row's
contribution to $G$ changes.  We used 41 angles in $[0,\pi/2]$, a penalty
selected on a 281-point grid $\alpha\in[10^{-6},10^{8}]$ by the DE
leave-one-out prediction risk, 500 shared exact ridge trials, and a
200,000-draw Gaussian distributional surrogate
(App.~\ref{sec:app-ratio-gaussian}) for the control ratios.

\paragraph{Recordings and stimulus sets.}
We reanalyzed the recordings and encoding-model outputs of \citetPW using the
authors' released preprocessing code (commit
\texttt{9b6fd95228d1b77146f8b279c7c228914ca34bb8}).  The analysis contains five
selected sites from each of five macaques ($25$ sites total): anterior IT in
Monkey Red, central IT in Monkey Paul, three V3 and two V4 sites in Monkey
Venus, and superior temporal sulcus sites in Monkeys Leap and Three0.  Red and
Paul were recorded with chronic 64-channel arrays; Venus, Leap, and Three0
were recorded with Neuropixels probes.  Site selection followed the reliability
screening of the original study.  Area and subject are consequently partly
confounded and we do not interpret area differences in the present analysis.

The encoding session contained 969 natural images: 649 images from the NSD
shared-image set, 259 segmented object and animal images, and 61 fLoc images.
A common fixed split assigned 774 images to model fitting and 195 to held-out
evaluation.  Control sessions were collected on 4--12 May 2025.  For every
site and encoding model, stimuli were synthesized from ten held-out NSD seed
images toward as many as 11 evenly spaced target responses.  Targets spanned
the measured encoding-response range, from the first percentile minus one
quarter bin width to the 99th percentile plus one half bin width.  Depending
on recording coverage, 36--110 accentuated images were available per
site--model pair (median 102).  The control sessions also repeated natural
images shared with the encoding session.  The held-out subset used below
contained 50 images for Red, Paul, and Venus, 24 for Leap, and 22 for Three0;
the complementary fitting-anchor counts were 50, 50, 50, 25, and 24.

\paragraph{Neural preprocessing.}
We used the response windows and exclusion rules of \citetPW.  Responses were
averaged over 100--400\,ms after image onset for Red and Paul and over
80--250\,ms for Venus, Leap, and Three0.  Within each control day, trials with
robust deviation $|y-\operatorname{median}(y)|/(1.4826\operatorname{MAD})>15$
were removed.  The remaining responses were standardized using that day's
natural-image anchors; a day with fewer than five anchor trials used all of
that day's trials.  Responses were then averaged over stimulus repetitions.
Near-duplicate Leap stimuli were merged using the released pixel-correlation
criterion ($r\geq0.90$).  At comparison time, predicted responses were lower
bounded by the fitted neuron's firing floor, defined by the original pipeline.

\paragraph{Cross-session response alignment.}
The preceding day-wise anchor standardization is part of the upstream
pipeline, but it does not place the encoding and control recordings on an
identical affine scale.  We therefore added one downstream map per site,
shared by all ten models.  On natural anchors belonging to the 774-image
fitting split, we fitted
\begin{equation}
 y^{\rm ctrl}_{is}=a_s+b_s y^{\rm enc}_{is}+\epsilon_{is}.
 \label{eq:app-cross-session-affine}
\end{equation}
The fitted $(a_s,b_s)$ was then frozen.  A model prediction $\hat y_{ism}$ was
mapped as
$\hat y^{\rm ctrl}_{ism}=a_s+b_s\max\{\hat y_{ism},f_s\}$, where $f_s$ is the
site's firing floor.  No accentuated response entered this fit, and the same
map was used for every model at a site.  Held-out natural-image MSE,
$E_{\rm val}$, was evaluated on the disjoint control-session anchors listed
above.  The control MSE was the mean squared difference between mapped
predictions and measured accentuated responses, without outcome refitting.
Control slope was the ordinary-least-squares coefficient in the regression of
measured on mapped predicted responses, with an intercept, over all available
accentuated stimuli for that site--model pair.  We also retained Pearson
correlation and identity
$R^2=1-\sum_i(y_i-\hat y_i)^2/\sum_i(y_i-\bar y)^2$.

\paragraph{Stimulus synthesis.}
Accentuation used the released Fourier-parameterized feature-visualization
procedure \citep{hamblin2024feature}: a $1024$-pixel image parameterization
with inverse-frequency preconditioning, random crops and injected noise was
optimized with NAdam (learning rate 12) for at most 6000 steps.  The iterate whose post-hoc model prediction came closest to the requested
target was retained.  A prespecified ladder of augmentation-noise and Fourier-decay
settings was searched independently for each site--model axis, as described by
\citetPW.

\subsection{Encoding models}
\label{sec:app-methods-models}
% Content: Table~\ref{tab:app-models}.  Loaders:
% Closed-loop-visual-insilico/core/model_load_utils.py:load_model_transform.
% Preprocessing: resize 224 + ImageNet norm (RN50, robust RN50, DINO RN50,
% DINOv2, AlexNet); RADIO resize only; CLIP-RN50, CLIPAG, SigLIP2, RegNetY:
% library eval transforms (NB: resnet50_clip used ImageNet norm at synthesis
% but CLIP transform at fitting -- upstream inconsistency, footnote?).
% Fitting: flattened layer activations -> PCA 750 (fit on 774 train images,
% centered, not whitened) -> sklearn RidgeCV(alphas = 14 log-spaced in
% [1e-4, 1e9], efficient LOO, alpha per channel); layer chosen per channel by
% held-out (195-image) R^2 -- the same split later reports encoding r.
% Candidate layers: Bottleneck blocks (ResNets, RegNet), ReLU/Conv/MaxPool
% (AlexNet), transformer blocks (ViTs; + attention pool for SigLIP2).
For each backbone and candidate layer, we flattened the layer activation and
fitted a 750-component PCA transform on the 774 encoding-training images.  PCA
scores were centered but not whitened.  A separate linear readout was fitted
for every neural site using \texttt{RidgeCV} with an intercept and
$\alpha\in\{10^{-4},10^{-3},\ldots,10^9\}$.  The implementation used efficient
leave-one-out ridge cross-validation to select $\alpha$ independently for each
response channel.  Candidate layers were ResNet and RegNet bottleneck blocks,
AlexNet convolution/ReLU/max-pool stages, and transformer blocks (including
the SigLIP2 attention pool).  For each site and backbone, the layer with the
largest $R^2$ on the fixed 195-image split was exported.  Thus this split was
used both for layer selection and for the reported encoding accuracy; its MSE
is a post-selection validation measure rather than an untouched final test
set.

readout-specific regularization and biological outcomes remained at the
250-row level.  Model loading, preprocessing, and layer names were taken from
the released experimental utilities and reproduced with the same checkpoints.

\subsection{Computing the gradient-geometry diagnostic}
\label{sec:app-methods-nonlinear}
% Used by: Fig.~\ref{fig:nonlinear-biological-validation},
% App.~\ref{sec:app-ext-nonlinear}.  Formulas and estimator definitions:
% App.~\ref{sec:app-nonlinear-diagnostic} (Eqs.~\ref{eq:nonlinear-local-fragility},
% \ref{eq:nonlinear-neighborhood-estimators}, \ref{eq:nonlinear-stein-estimator}).
% Content: kappa from the CV alpha and the training PCA spectrum
% (kappa(1 - df_1/n) = alpha/n, n = 774); one VJP per PC (750, batched);
% 224x224 RGB grid in [0,1] units (d = 150,528); average over the 10 seeds;
% neighborhood scales tau in {0.5, 2, 8, 16}/255, R = 64 directions,
% L = 8 centers; debiased U-statistics; finite-difference check; antithetic
% Stein with R = 512; E_val; V_phi = E_val T / n.  Checks: 84 unique
% model--layer geometries, residual <= 6e-8; compute ~1.4 H100-hours.
For a model--layer geometry, let $s_k$ be the variance of PCA score $k$ on the
774 fitting images and let $\alpha_{sm}$ be the selected scikit-learn ridge
penalty for site $s$ and model $m$.  Because scikit-learn minimizes an
unnormalized residual sum of squares, we set $\lambda_{sm}=\alpha_{sm}/n$ with
$n=774$ and solved the positive fixed-point equation
\begin{equation}
 \kappa_{sm}\left[1-\frac{1}{n}\sum_{k=1}^{750}
 \frac{s_k}{s_k+\kappa_{sm}}\right]=\lambda_{sm}.
 \label{eq:app-alpha-to-kappa}
\end{equation}
The 750-dimensional training spectrum is used here as a plug-in approximation
to the population spectrum.  Across the 250 readouts, the median solved
$\kappa$ was 3.48 and the median
$\mathrm{df}_2=\sum_k[s_k/(s_k+\kappa)]^2$ was 231.5.

We defined the differentiable input at the $224\times224$ RGB grid, before
model-specific channel normalization, so $d=150{,}528$ and derivatives are in
RGB units on $[0,1]$.  The upstream resize from the synthesized image was not
differentiated.  For each of the ten fixed seed images and every PCA coordinate
$k$, reverse-mode automatic differentiation gave
\begin{equation}
 q_k(x)=\|J_\phi(x)^\top u_k\|_2^2,
 \qquad
 T_\phi(x,\kappa)=\sum_{k=1}^{750}
 q_k(x)\frac{s_k}{(s_k+\kappa)^2}.
 \label{eq:app-neural-trace-computation}
\end{equation}
The 750 vector--Jacobian products were evaluated in bounded blocks (up to 32
PCA directions per backward pass) and averaged over seeds.  The primary
theory-motivated proxy was
$V_{\phi,sm}=E_{{\rm val},sm}\,\mathbb E_x[T_\phi(x,\kappa_{sm})]/n$, using the
held-out control-session anchor MSE from
Eq.~\ref{eq:app-cross-session-affine}.

We additionally evaluated finite-neighborhood versions at per-channel Gaussian
noise scales $\tau\in\{0.5,2,8,16\}/255$.  Perturbed RGB values were not
clipped.  Each seed used $R=64$ Gaussian outer directions and $L=8$
independent neighborhood centers.  The smoothed-Jacobian estimator targeted
$\|\mathbb E_z J_\phi(x+\tau z)^\top u_k\|^2$ and removed finite-$L$ square
bias with the off-diagonal U-statistic of
Eq.~\ref{eq:nonlinear-neighborhood-estimators}.  The neighborhood estimator
targeted $\mathbb E_z\|J_\phi(x+\tau z)^\top u_k\|^2$.  We also retained the
centered response covariance, total finite response change, its odd/even
decomposition, and a local symmetric finite-difference check.  Monte Carlo
summaries were formed at the outer-direction level before summing over PCs, so
cross-PC covariance was retained.  As a separate forward-only validation, an
antithetic Stein estimator used $R=512$ common-prefix directions and replaced
$f(x+\tau z)-f(x)$ by
$[f(x+\tau z)-f(x-\tau z)]/2$ to cancel even-response variance.

All spectral vectors were stored before $\kappa$ weighting, allowing traces to
be recomputed without rerunning a network.  The production run covered 84/84
unique geometries and 840 geometry--seed archives.  Exact PCA-cache
reproduction, serial-versus-batched VJPs, and the identities ``variance plus
drift equals total'' and ``odd plus even equals total'' were checked; the
largest relative decomposition residual was $5.96\times10^{-8}$.  Nine
CLIP-RN50 geometries had marginal finite-difference diagnostics, so their
step-dependent smooth and neighborhood estimates were retained as
sensitivities; exact VJPs and finite-response covariance do not depend on that
step size.  Total production compute was estimated at approximately 1.4 H100 GPU-hours.

\subsection{Neuronal peer-review analysis}
\label{sec:app-methods-peer}
% Used by: Sec.~\ref{sec:phenomena}, peer paragraph of Sec.~\ref{sec:pixel},
% Fig.~\ref{fig:supp-neural-peer-matrix}, Fig.~\ref{fig:supp-neural-self-vs-peer}.
% Content: 25 sites x 10 generators x 10 reviewers; reviewer prediction on
% the generator's stimuli through the same frozen affine map; MSE, r,
% identity R^2, slope; equal-weight site means; self (diagonal) vs.\ peer
% tests: reviewer-label permutation, Wilcoxon over generators, subject
% sign-flip, subject bootstrap.
For every site $s$ and generator model $g$, we selected the personalized
accentuated stimuli generated for that site's recorded unit.  Each of the ten
reviewer models $r$ predicted the identical stimulus set.  Reviewer predictions
were firing-floor clamped and transformed using the same frozen site-level
map $(a_s,b_s)$ as the self prediction.  This yielded
$25\times10\times10=2500$ site--generator--reviewer cells; rows of the displayed
matrices are generators, columns are reviewers, and diagonal cells are
self-evaluations.  Within each cell we computed MSE, Pearson $r$, identity
$R^2$, and the OLS slope of measured on reviewer-predicted responses.  Metrics
were first computed within site and then averaged with equal weight over the
25 sites; stimuli were never pooled across sites.  Available stimulus counts,
total trials, and mean repeats were retained for every cell.

For the self-versus-peer comparison, the 10 diagonal and 90 off-diagonal cells
of the 25-site-averaged matrix were summarized by medians and interquartile
ranges.  These cell counts are descriptive because rows and columns share
models.  Dependence-aware inference used three complementary contrasts.  First,
within each generator row we compared its diagonal value with the mean of its
nine peer reviewers and applied a paired Wilcoxon test over ten generators.
Second, we permuted the global reviewer labels 200,000 times while preserving
the complete matrix rows and columns, testing whether the named self diagonal
was unusually separated from a randomly matched diagonal.  Third, we formed a
peer-minus-self contrast within each site, averaged the five sites within each
subject, and used 100,000 equal-subject bootstrap resamples for confidence
intervals.  With only five subjects, we also report the exact two-sided
subject sign-flip test, whose minimum attainable $p$ value when all subjects
agree is 0.0625.  For slope, signed deviation from one and
$|\mathrm{slope}-1|$ were kept separate: a change from slope below one to above
one is not by itself improved calibration.

\subsection{Statistical analysis}
\label{sec:app-methods-stats}
% Content: log predictors; within-site centering of predictor and outcome;
% Pearson / Spearman / site-clustered OLS; model subsets 10/9/8/7;
% Benjamini--Hochberg within endpoint x subset; image bootstrap;
% leave-one-monkey-out / leave-one-model-out; repeated-measures caveat.
The biological synopsis contains one row per site and model ($25\times10=250$).
Positive geometry and error predictors were analyzed on a base-10 logarithmic
scale.  For Fig.~\ref{fig:nonlinear-biological-validation}, the biological
control slope was centered within site using the mean of all ten models,
\begin{equation}
 y^{\circ}_{sm}=y_{sm}-\frac{1}{10}\sum_{m'=1}^{10}y_{sm'},
 \label{eq:app-site-centered-control}
\end{equation}
whereas the displayed predictors remained at their raw log scale.  Subset
comparisons used all ten models; the nine trained models; eight models after
removing RN50-Robust and CLIPAG; and seven models after additionally removing
the untrained AlexNet.  The site means in
Eq.~\ref{eq:app-site-centered-control} were estimated once from all ten models
and held fixed before subset filtering.  Direction-aligned correlations were
reported as $-r$ for control slope, because the theory predicts that larger
control-error proxies accompany smaller slopes.

The figure reports ordinary two-sided Pearson correlations and marks raw
$p<0.05$ as exploratory.  The 250 site--model rows are repeated measurements,
so these pointwise $p$ values are not treated as independent-sample
confirmatory tests.  Companion tables report Spearman correlations and linear
regressions with site-clustered standard errors.  A sensitivity analysis also
removed each site's mean from the log predictor; another recomputed site means
within each 10/9/8/7-model subset.  For the complete 28-predictor benchmark,
Benjamini--Hochberg correction was applied within each biological endpoint and
model subset; the paper-facing seven-predictor panel displays the unadjusted
threshold and the full FDR view is reported separately.

Finite-image uncertainty in $E_{\rm val}$ was assessed with 2000 bootstrap
resamples.  Held-out image identities were resampled jointly for every
site--model row within a monkey, preserving the shared-stimulus dependence;
this procedure quantifies image-set uncertainty but not uncertainty across
animals, neural trials, or Jacobian probes.  As an out-of-sample sensitivity,
fixed log-linear predictors of control slope were evaluated by leave-one-model-
out and leave-one-subject-out prediction.  Predictor standardization and all
regression coefficients were fitted within each training fold, with no
hyperparameter search on held-out outcomes.  Throughout, we emphasize effect
sizes, model-subset sensitivity, and held-out comparisons because only five
subjects contribute independent animal-level replication.

\subsection{Software and compute}
\label{sec:app-methods-compute}
\red{Experiments were implemented in Python~3.10 using PyTorch~2.4,
NumPy~2.0, SciPy~1.14, pandas~2.2, scikit-learn~1.6, and
statsmodels~0.14; figures and progress monitoring used Matplotlib~3.8 and
\texttt{tqdm}.  Linear-feature and null-rotation simulations used the measured
$d=10{,}000$ natural-image spectrum, $n=1{,}000$ samples, and feature dimension
up to $p=1{,}000$ ($p=500$ in the main construction).  These simulations ran
on CPU and required less than five CPU-minutes in aggregate; the
full-dimensional PCA/whitening check required less than one minute on a single
NVIDIA H100 80-GB GPU.}

\red{The largest pixel-space landscape comprised 100 paired natural-image
trials, 114 noise levels, and 478 ridge values.  Exact reuse of ridge-path
coefficients across noise levels reduced this to one factorization per trial;
including Gaussian-surrogate evaluation, the landscape required approximately
one H100 GPU-minute.  The nonlinear gradient-geometry analysis evaluated 10
pretrained encoders at 84 unique layer geometries (250 site--model pairs),
using 750 feature PCs and 10 seed images.  Batched vector--Jacobian products
and finite-neighborhood diagnostics required approximately two recorded H100
GPU-hours, or a few GPU-hours including validation and reruns.  Intermediate
arrays and plot-ready summaries were cached separately so that figures could
be regenerated without recomputing model gradients; the principal cached
outputs occupied approximately 6~GB, excluding source datasets and pretrained
model weights.}
